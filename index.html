About Freedom Cup

Everything you need to know about the tournament

The Freedom Cup Story

Cricket has always been more than a sport — it's a bond that unites communities across cultures. The Freedom Cup brings that spirit to New Haven, Connecticut on America's Independence Day. Ten teams, one shared love for the game, and one action-packed day of thrilling tape ball cricket under the summer sun. Whether you grew up playing gully cricket, backyard tape ball, or discovered the sport right here in the USA — this tournament is your stage. Fast-paced, affordable, and open to all skill levels. No expensive gear, no barriers — just pure cricketing joy, sixes flying, community pride, and the pursuit of glory. 🏏🇺🇸

Format Overview

  • 9-a-side, 6 overs per side
  • Match duration: ~60 minutes
  • 15-minute break between rounds

Tournament Structure

  • 2 Pools × 5 Teams
  • 4 pool matches per team
  • Top 2 per pool → Semi-finals
  • Winners → Grand Final

Venue

Rules & Regulations

Complete tournament rulebook

Official Match Umpires Hired

Professional, neutral umpires have been appointed for all matches to ensure fair play, unbiased decisions, and the highest standard of game integrity. No team-affiliated umpiring — every decision is impartial.

  • All decisions by the tournament committee are final.
  • Teams must report 15 minutes before scheduled match time.
  • A team failing to field the minimum players within 10 minutes forfeits the match.
  • Taped tennis balls provided by organizers will be used for all matches.
  • Teams must bring their own bats (no professional/hard-ball bats). Organizers will not provide bats.
  • No metal spikes or studs allowed.
  • Each team must designate a captain who will be the point of contact.

  • LBW is NOT applicable (no pads required in tape ball cricket).
  • Catches must be taken cleanly (ball caught without bouncing — full catch only).
  • Batsman is out if the ball hits the wicket directly or via deflection off the batsman's body.
  • Retired batsman may return only after all other batsmen are dismissed.
  • Running between wickets: standard cricket rules apply.
  • Boundary: 4 runs if ball crosses along ground; 6 runs if ball clears boundary on the full.

  • Bowling allocation: 2, 2, 1, 1 overs — must use 4 bowlers to complete 6 overs. Two bowlers bowl 2 overs each, two bowlers bowl 1 over each. No bowler can bowl more than 2 overs.
  • No-ball: Full toss above waist height, front-foot overstepping.
  • Wide: Ball passing outside leg or off stump beyond batsman's reach. Umpire's discretion.
  • No-ball awards 1 extra run + free hit (next delivery, batsman can only be run out).
  • Wide awards 1 extra run + re-bowl.
  • Underarm bowling is not allowed.

  • Catches must be taken on the full (no bounce) — one hand or two hands, both valid.
  • If the ball bounces before being caught, it is NOT out.
  • Boundary catch: fielder must not move or carry momentum beyond the boundary line while completing the catch — feet must remain inside the field of play.
  • No fielder may stand within 5 yards of the batsman (except wicketkeeper and slip).
  • Substitutes allowed for injury only, with umpire's permission (sub cannot bat or bowl).

  • Time Limit: Bowling team must complete 6 overs within 27 minutes.
  • Penalty: 5 runs awarded to the batting team for each over not bowled within the time limit.
  • Time starts from the first ball of the innings.
  • Stoppage time (injuries, lost ball) excluded at umpire's discretion.

If teams are tied on points at the end of pool stage:

  1. Net Run Rate (NRR) — higher NRR ranks above.
  2. Head-to-Head — result of the match between the tied teams.
  3. Coin Toss — as a last resort, supervised by the tournament committee.

Knockout match tie: Super Over. If still tied, boundary count. If still tied, coin toss.

  • Spirit of cricket must be upheld at all times.
  • Abusive language, sledging, or aggressive behavior will result in a warning; repeat offense leads to player suspension.
  • Respect umpires, opponents, and spectators.
  • No alcohol or substance use during matches.
  • Any disputes must be raised by the captain only, to the tournament committee.
  • Physical altercation results in immediate disqualification of the player and potential team disqualification.
